# Oakville Dads Meeting Strategy Guide
## Bronte Harbour Classic 5-10k Race - Charity Partnership Discussion

**Meeting Date**: TBD
**Attendees**: Greg, Charles, and 3 main Oakville Dads representatives (including Peter)
**Duration**: 60-90 minutes
**Location**: Global Citizens office on Spears

## MEETING OBJECTIVES

### Primary Goals:
1. ✅ Establish Oakville Dads as the official charity partner for the race
2. ✅ Secure volunteer support from their membership base
3. ✅ Gain marketing support and promotional help
4. ✅ Create a mutually beneficial partnership without guaranteed financial commitments

### Secondary Goals:
- Build long-term relationship for annual event partnership
- Generate excitement about Father's Day race alignment
- Understand their expectations and capacity
- Explore creative fundraising opportunities beyond registration donations

**Opening Positioning** (Start with this):

The alignment is perfect - a Father's Day community running event partnered with an organization dedicated to supporting fathers in our community. It just makes sense."

**For Oakville Dads:**
- ✅ Father's Day = Perfect brand alignment
- ✅ Community visibility at large public event
- ✅ Direct access to hundreds of families and potential members
- ✅ Multiple revenue generation opportunities
- ✅ Positive community association with health/wellness

**For Bronte Harbour Classic:**
- ✅ Meaningful local charity partner (not generic charity)

- ✅ Enhanced community credibility

## WHAT WE'RE OFFERING (The Partnership Package)

### 1. Registration Donation Option
**What It Is**:
- Every person who registers for the race will see an optional donation field
- Donations go directly to Oakville Dads charitable number
- Promoted as "Supporting Oakville Dads" throughout registration process

**Talking Point**:
> "Every single person who registers will have the opportunity to support Oakville Dads. We're estimating 500-1,000 participants for our first year, and if even a portion make donations, that's significant support for your cause."

### 2. On-Site Fundraising Opportunities
**What We're Providing**:
- **Booth/Tent Space**: Prime location at race venue (we have the park for extended time)
- **Raffle Table**: Space to run raffles (participants love raffles)
- **Merchandise Sales**: Can sell Oakville Dads merchandise/gear
- **Membership Recruitment**: Direct access to families on Father's Day
- **Information Distribution**: Hand out materials, talk to attendees

**Talking Point**:
> "You'll have prime real estate at the event with hundreds of families coming through. Many charity partners at races make significant money through raffles, merchandise, and on-site engagement - often more than registration donations."

### 3. Charity Challenge Program
**What It Is**:
- Encourage running clubs, corporate teams, and groups to fundraise for Oakville Dads
- Similar to marathon charity runner programs
- Groups create fundraising pages and compete to raise the most
- Provides recognition for top fundraising teams

**Talking Point**:
> "We want to create a charity challenge where teams compete to raise the most money for Oakville Dads. This taps into the competitive spirit of runners while supporting your mission. We've seen other races raise thousands this way."

### 4. Dog Walk Component (Bonus Idea)
**Partnership with Bone & Biscuit**:
- Father's Day 1K Dog Walk
- Separate registration (lower price point)
- 100% of proceeds go to Oakville Dads
- Sponsored by Bone & Biscuit (local pet store already interested)

**Talking Point**:
> "We're exploring adding a 1K Father's Day Dog Walk in partnership with Bone & Biscuit. This would be a separate event where all proceeds go directly to Oakville Dads. Lots of dads want to bring their dogs on Father's Day, and this gives families another way to participate and donate."

### 5. Marketing and Promotional Value
**What Oakville Dads Gets**:
- Logo on all race materials (website, posters, social media, emails)
- Mentioned in all media coverage and press releases
- Recognition in race announcements (before/during/after race)
- Photo opportunities with race winners and medal presentations
- Content for your social media and newsletters
- Association with growing annual community event

**Talking Point**:
> "Your organization will be front and center in all our race promotion. That's valuable exposure to the broader Oakville community, not just your current members. As this race grows year over year, that visibility grows with it."

### 6. Long-Term Partnership Opportunity
**The Vision**:
- Year 1: Prove the concept, build the foundation
- Year 2+: With Year 1 data, can discuss guaranteed minimum donations
- Annual Father's Day tradition that grows each year
- Potential to expand (more race distances, more activities)

**Talking Point**:
> "This isn't a one-time thing. We're building an annual Father's Day tradition in Oakville. As the race grows, the benefit to Oakville Dads grows. By Year 2, we'll have real data and can discuss more structured financial commitments."

Having Oakville Dads volunteers not only helps us run a great event, but it gives your members a way to be involved and visible in the community. Can we count on volunteer support from your membership?"

### 2. Marketing and Promotional Support
**What We Need**:
- Share race information with Oakville Dads membership
- Post about race on Oakville Dads social media
- Include race info in newsletters/communications
- Encourage members to participate and register

**How to Ask**:
> "We'd love your help spreading the word about the race to your membership and through your social media channels. Many of your members would be perfect participants, and the more people who know about it, the bigger the event becomes - which means more fundraising potential for Oakville Dads."

### 3. Active Participation in Fundraising
**What We Need**:
- Designate point person from Oakville Dads to coordinate

- Organize raffle items and merchandise

**How to Ask**:

## WHAT WE'RE NOT COMMITTING TO (CRITICAL BOUNDARIES)

### ❌ NO Guaranteed Donation Amounts

**Why**:
- This is our first year - we don't know if we'll break even or make profit
- Cannot commit to financial amounts we can't guarantee
- Need to ensure race sustainability for future years
- Must cover all race expenses first

**If They Ask for Guaranteed Amount, Say This**:
> "We completely understand wanting to know potential financial impact, but here's our honest situation: this is our first year running this race. We don't know yet if we'll break even or generate profit. Our priority is making sure the race is successful and sustainable so it can continue and grow. What we CAN guarantee is that we'll provide every opportunity for Oakville Dads to raise money through registrations, on-site activities, and fundraising challenges. We can't guarantee a specific dollar amount from our profits, but we can guarantee significant fundraising opportunities and a platform to engage with the community."

**Follow-Up**:
> "Here's our proposal: Let's make Year 1 about proving the concept and building the foundation. We'll track all the donation data, fundraising results, and participation numbers. Then, for Year 2 and beyond, we'll have real data to work with and can discuss more structured financial commitments if it makes sense. Would that work for you?"

### ❌ NO Percentage of Profits Commitment (Year 1)

**Why**:
- Don't know what profits will be (if any)
- Need financial flexibility to reinvest in race growth
- Many first-year races break even or lose money
- Setting precedent that's hard to change later

**If They Push for Percentage, Say This**:
> "We're not in a position to commit a percentage of profits this year because we honestly don't know what the financial picture will look like. What we're offering instead is something potentially more valuable: direct access to participants for donations, prime space for fundraising activities, and all the promotional value of being our charity partner. Many charity partners at established races make more money from on-site activities than they do from profit-sharing."

### ❌ NO Open-Ended Financial Obligations

**Why**:
- Could jeopardize race financial viability
- Need to maintain control of race finances
- Uncertain costs and potential overruns

**If They Want Ongoing Financial Commitment, Say This**:
> "We need to be financially responsible with this first year. That means ensuring we can cover all race costs, deliver a great experience, and have resources to grow the event. We're offering a partnership with fundraising opportunities, not a financial sponsorship arrangement. If the race is successful and profitable, we'd be happy to discuss what future years could look like."

## CONVERSATION FLOW (Recommended Structure)

### PHASE 1: Opening and Building Rapport (10 minutes)

**Start Positive and Enthusiastic**:
1. Thank them for meeting
2. Express excitement about Father's Day alignment
3. Share vision for the race becoming an annual tradition
4. Acknowledge their work in the community

**Example Opening**:
> "Thanks so much for meeting with us. We're really excited to talk about this partnership because the fit is so natural - Father's Day race and Oakville Dads just makes perfect sense. We're building what we hope will become an annual Oakville tradition, and having you as our charity partner would make it even more meaningful."

### PHASE 2: Understanding Their Needs (15 minutes)

**Ask Questions BEFORE Presenting Your Offer**:

1. **"Tell us about Oakville Dads and your mission."**
   - Let them talk about their organization
   - Shows you care about understanding them
   - Gives you information to tailor your pitch

2. **"What are your typical fundraising activities throughout the year?"**
   - Understand their fundraising experience
   - Learn what works well for them
   - Identify gaps this race could fill

3. **"What would make a charity partnership like this successful from your perspective?"**
   - Critical question - learn their expectations upfront
   - Helps you address their needs directly
   - May reveal dealbreakers early

4. **"How many active members do you have who might be interested in volunteering?"**
   - Gauge volunteer capacity
   - Shows you value their involvement beyond money
   - Helps them see concrete way to participate

5. **"Have you partnered with community events like this before? What worked well and what didn't?"**
   - Learn from their past experiences
   - Avoid mistakes others made
   - Build on successful models

**Listen Carefully and Take Notes** - their answers will guide the rest of the conversation.

### PHASE 3: Presenting the Partnership Opportunity (20 minutes)

**Structure Your Pitch Around What They Said They Need**:

1. **Start with the Perfect Fit Story** (see section above)

2. **Present the Partnership Package** (see "What We're Offering")
   - Registration donations
   - On-site fundraising opportunities
   - Charity challenge
   - Marketing value
   - Long-term vision

3. **Paint the Picture of Race Day**:
> "Picture this: Father's Day morning, beautiful weather, hundreds of families at Bronte Harbour. Your booth is set up right near the finish line. Dads are crossing the finish line with their kids, they're in a great mood, and they see your booth. You've got raffle tickets for sale, merchandise, information about your programs. Meanwhile, throughout the morning, our announcer is mentioning Oakville Dads, thanking you for being our charity partner. Every finisher who made a donation during registration gets recognized. Your logo is on the race shirts. Local media is covering the event and interviewing your volunteers. That's the experience we're creating."

4. **Address the Financial Reality Proactively**:
> "Now, I want to be completely transparent about the financial side. This is our first year, and we honestly don't know if we'll break even or generate profit. We have to cover significant costs: permits, insurance, timing equipment, medals, shirts, marketing, and more. So we're not in a position to guarantee a specific donation amount from race profits. What we ARE guaranteeing is every opportunity for Oakville Dads to fundraise directly - through registrations, on-site activities, and fundraising challenges. We believe that approach has more potential anyway, because you're engaging directly with participants rather than just receiving a check at the end."

### PHASE 4: Discussing What You Need (10 minutes)

**Present Your Needs as Partnership Requirements**:

> "For this partnership to work well for both of us, here's what we'd need from Oakville Dads:"

1. Volunteer support on race day
2. Marketing help to spread the word
3. Active engagement in fundraising (staffing booth, organizing raffles)
4. Charitable registration number for tax receipts

**Frame It Positively**:
> "The more Oakville Dads puts into this event, the more you'll get out of it. The races that raise the most money for their charity partners are the ones where the charity is actively engaged, bringing energy and enthusiasm, not just passively receiving donations."

### PHASE 5: Addressing Concerns and Objections (15 minutes)

**Be Ready for These Common Objections**:

#### Objection 1: "We need to know how much money we'll receive."

**Response**:
> "We totally understand that, and if this was an established race, we could give you that data. But in Year 1, we'd be making up numbers. What we CAN tell you is that similar races with active charity partners typically see $5-20 per registered participant in donations, plus whatever is raised on-site. With 500-1,000 expected participants, that could be $2,500-$20,000 or more - but these are estimates, not guarantees. The actual amount depends partly on how actively the charity engages with participants. What we're offering is the opportunity and platform. The fundraising results depend on both of us working together."

#### Objection 2: "Other races give us a percentage of profits."

**Response**:
> "That's great, and if this race is successful, we'd be happy to discuss that model for Year 2 and beyond. But those other races probably have track records and can predict their financials. We're in startup mode. We think the direct fundraising model is actually better for you anyway - you're not dependent on us having a profitable year, you're controlling your own fundraising destiny. And you're building relationships directly with race participants, which has value beyond just the money raised."

#### Objection 3: "This sounds like a lot of work for uncertain return."

**Response**:
> "Fair point. Let's talk about effort vs. return. On race day, you'd need volunteers for maybe 4-5 hours. Leading up to race day, maybe 5-10 hours of planning and preparation. So call it 15 hours total of volunteer time. If you raise $5,000-$10,000 (which is very realistic), that's incredible return on time invested. Plus, you get marketing exposure to the whole community, potential new members, and you're associated with a positive, healthy, family-friendly event on Father's Day. That's hard to put a dollar value on."

#### Objection 4: "We're already stretched thin with other commitments."

**Response**:
> "We hear you, and we don't want to add burden to your members. Here's the thing: the race is on Father's Day anyway. What we're offering is a way for your members who are already interested in community events to participate in something that directly benefits your organization. You're not creating a new event - we are. You're piggybacking on our work. We're looking for 30-50 volunteers ideally, but we understand Father's Day timing means some of your members won't be available. If you could provide 30 volunteers, that would be excellent, and we can supplement with volunteers from schools and other community sources. We're being realistic about the commitment and flexible about the numbers."

#### Objection 5: "What if the race doesn't happen or gets cancelled?"

**Response**:
> "That's a legitimate concern. Here's what we can tell you: we're already 75+ registrations in (and it's only January/February), we have our permits in process with the Town of Oakville, we have sponsors lined up, and we're committed to making this happen. The only scenario where the race wouldn't happen is a major unforeseen event - weather emergency, public health crisis, etc. And if that happened, nobody would hold that against Oakville Dads - you'd just be associated with an event that had to postpone. But we're confident this race is happening June 15th."

### PHASE 6: The Ask and Next Steps (10 minutes)

**If the Conversation is Going Well**:

> "So, what do you think? Does this sound like a partnership that makes sense for Oakville Dads? We're excited about the potential, and we think the Father's Day alignment is too perfect to pass up. Can we move forward with Oakville Dads as our official charity partner?"

**If They Seem Hesitant**:

> "We can see you might have some reservations. What are the main concerns you have? Let's talk through them and see if there's a way to make this work for everyone."

**Suggested Next Steps** (If They Say Yes):

1. ✅ **Week 1**: Oakville Dads provides charitable registration number and legal contact
2. ✅ **Week 2**: Draft simple partnership agreement outlining mutual commitments
3. ✅ **Week 3**: Begin integrating Oakville Dads into race marketing materials
4. ✅ **Week 4**: Kickoff call to plan specific fundraising activities and volunteer coordination
5. ✅ **Ongoing**: Monthly check-ins leading up to race day

**Get Contact Information**:
- Primary point person for partnership
- Email and phone for coordination
- Best way to reach them for quick questions

### PHASE 7: Closing (5 minutes)

**End on a Positive, Forward-Looking Note**:

> "We really appreciate you taking the time to meet with us today. We're building something special with this race, and having Oakville Dads as our charity partner would make it even more meaningful. We think this is the beginning of a great annual tradition in Oakville - a Father's Day race that brings the community together and supports fathers in our area. Let's make it happen!"

## NEGOTIATION GUIDELINES

### What You CAN Agree To in the Meeting:

✅ Registration donation option with their charitable number
✅ Booth/tent space at the event
✅ Logo on race materials and marketing
✅ Recognition in announcements and media
✅ Supporting their on-site fundraising activities
✅ Charity challenge program
✅ Marketing cross-promotion
✅ Long-term partnership vision

### What You CANNOT Agree To Without Further Discussion:

❌ Guaranteed dollar amounts from race profits
❌ Percentage of race profits (Year 1)
❌ Exclusive charity partner restrictions (that prevent other sponsors)
❌ Oakville Dads having approval over race decisions
❌ Covering costs for their activities (they cover their own raffle prizes, booth materials, etc.)
❌ Giving them revenue share from vendor fees or sponsorships

### When to Use "Let Me Discuss That With My Partner":

If they ask for something you're unsure about, don't say yes in the meeting. Use this phrase:

> "That's an interesting idea. Greg and I need to discuss that together and look at the financial implications. Can we get back to you on that within a few days?"

### Red Flags (Warning Signs to Walk Away):

🚩 They demand guaranteed minimum donation amounts as requirement
🚩 They want percentage of all race revenue (not just profits)
🚩 They want decision-making authority over race operations
🚩 They're not willing to provide volunteer support
🚩 They want upfront payment before race happens
🚩 They're dismissive or disrespectful of your first-year situation
🚩 They create complex legal requirements that jeopardize race

If you see multiple red flags, it's okay to walk away. Kerr Street Mission is a solid backup.
