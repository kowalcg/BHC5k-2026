## Methodology (read before adding rows)

The Creator Breakout Kit's core rule: **look at post-level performance
outliers, not creator follower counts.** A 2,000-follower account whose
single reel did 400K views is a stronger pattern signal than a 200K-follower
account whose average reel does 3K views.

### How to audit a post
1. Look at the post's **engagement ratio vs the account's normal.** If the
   post is >3× the account's median — that's an outlier worth logging.
2. Capture the **hook** (first 3 seconds of video / first line of caption).
3. Capture the **format** (reel, carousel, static, live, UGC repost).
4. Name the **pattern** — the *transferable* thing, not the specific post.
5. Rate **confidence**: Hypothesis (1 post) · Directional (2–3 posts) · Strong (4+ posts across accounts).

### Where to look
- **Direct comparables:** Toronto Waterfront Marathon, Oakville Half, Robbie
  Burns Road Race, Mississauga Marathon, Burlington Chilly Half, MEC Race
  Series, Rock 'n' Roll Running Series.
- **Adjacent:** Parkrun Canada posts, local running clubs (Bronte Runners,
  Longboat, Tribe Fitness), Father's Day family-event content, small-town
  festivals that pair with a race.
- **Internal proof:** our own `Toronto Waterfront Marathon 2025/` folder
  already has reference material from TWM — start there.

## Pattern log

### Pattern A — Drone/cinematic course reveal
- **Format:** 15–30s reel, aerial drone footage of the course, text overlay
  with course stats, music-forward (no VO).
- **Hook example:** "This is the first 500 metres of the race most runners
  don't know exists yet."
- **Why it works:** Saves + shares spike because people tag running friends;
  works for "should I travel for this?" objection (O14, O15).
- **Objections it addresses:** O6 (PR-able?), O14 (worth the drive?), O15
  (scenic?).
- **Production cost:** Medium — needs drone + edit. Myles volunteered for
  video content (see MKT-004).

### Pattern B — First-timer training diary (weekly)
- **Format:** Carousel or 30–60s reel. Real person, same face every week.
  "Week 4 of training for my first 5K." Messy, honest, no production polish.
- **Hook example:** "I'm 42, I've never run a 5K, I signed up for Bronte
  Harbour Classic anyway. Here's week 4."
- **Why it works:** Parasocial attachment compounds over a series. People
  come back to see if the person finishes. Drives FB group shares.
- **Objections:** O1 (not a real runner), O2 (5K is far), D1 (want to feel
  like a runner).
- **Production cost:** **Low.** Phone camera, one committed person, weekly
  cadence. This is the single highest-leverage play we have.
- **Confidence:** **Directional** — this pattern is well-documented in
  run-influencer spaces (Parkrun, Couch-to-5K communities). Needs local
  proof.

### Pattern C — Dad-and-kid duo training (Father's Day wedge)
- **Format:** 20–40s reel. Dad + kid(s) running together, intercut with
  morning-routine beats (lacing shoes, high-five, finish-line goal on the
  fridge). Emotional, earnest, zero irony.
- **Hook example:** "My daughter said she wanted to run a race with me for
  Father's Day. So we signed up for Bronte Harbour Classic."
- **Why it works:** This is the single most ownable emotional angle for this
  specific race. Father's Day + race + kids 1K = a combination no other local
  race can say.
- **Objections:** O8 (run *with* my kid), O9 (relaxing vs logistics), O10
  (kid overwhelmed), D2 (memory my kid talks about).
- **Production cost:** Low. Needs one committed dad-and-kid pair. Oakville
  Dads Charity is the partner that can surface candidates.
- **Confidence:** **Hypothesis** — needs to be tested by the Mother's Day
  equivalents from comparable races. Log them below.

### Pattern D — Countdown with price-tier urgency
- **Format:** Static or short reel. Stark text: "Premier pricing ends Dec 4.
  Early Bird starts Dec 5. $16.33 more per entry."
- **Hook example:** "You have 48 hours to save $16 on Bronte Harbour
  Classic."
- **Why it works:** Real deadline, real dollar amount, no fake urgency.
  Converts warm audiences who've been lurking.
- **Objections:** Tips O7 (why pay?) and O4 (worth it?) over the edge when
  they're already 80% ready.
- **Production cost:** Very low — design template, fill in dates.
- **Confidence:** **Strong** — this is conversion-marketing 101. The
  question isn't whether it works; it's whether we have the list to send it
  to.

### Pattern E — Course POV (GoPro handheld run-through)
- **Format:** 60–90s reel. Runner's-eye view of the entire course at race
  pace, with mile markers as text overlays. No music, or light ambient.
- **Hook example:** "The full 5K course, sub-30 pace, in under 90 seconds."
- **Why it works:** High completion rate on Reels/TikTok (POV is sticky),
  answers "what does it look like?" for everyone at once. Evergreen — can
  repost monthly.
- **Objections:** O5 (will it be chaos?), O6 (PR-able?), O15 (scenic?).
- **Production cost:** Medium. One person with a chest-mounted GoPro, one
  practice run on the official course once it's marked.
- **Confidence:** **Directional** — POV runs are a well-established format
  on YouTube and IG Reels for trail/road races.

### Pattern F — Local-business window/storefront takeover
- **Format:** Photo + short caption. A poster/flyer in a recognizable local
  business window (Starbucks Bronte, Second Cup, Peach Coffee Shop) with the
  business's own IG reposting.
- **Hook example:** "Spotted at @peachcoffeeshop — Father's Day is going to
  be a day."
- **Why it works:** Borrowed trust from established local brands, plus each
  business's followers = free organic reach. Already in the marketing tracker
  as MKT-006.
- **Objections:** O12 (is it actually good?), O13 (community-trust signal).
- **Production cost:** Very low — print cost + in-person ask.
- **Confidence:** **Directional** — proven pattern for local events.

### Pattern G — Behind-the-scenes build-out (inaugural-year content)
- **Format:** Short-form documentary cadence. "Day 112 before the first
  Bronte Harbour Classic." Greg + Bronte Runners committee scouting, hanging
  the sponsor wall, unboxing medals.
- **Hook example:** "These medals have never been given out before. June 21
  will be the first time."
- **Why it works:** Inaugural-year scarcity (T5). Builds parasocial
  attachment with the race itself, not just a runner. Makes Day-Of posts way
  more emotional because viewers feel invested.
- **Objections:** O5 (will it be chaos?) — flipping it into a virtue by
  *showing* the prep.
- **Production cost:** Low — phone-camera vertical video, 1 min per post,
  1–2x/week until race day.
- **Confidence:** **Directional** — "Day X of building Y" is a proven TikTok
  meta-format.

### Pattern H — Sponsor-activated UGC challenge
- **Format:** Branded hashtag + prompt. "Post your training run tagging
  #RoadToBronte and tag 3 friends — best 5 get bibs comped."
- **Hook example:** Give-away framing with sponsor logo attached.
- **Why it works:** Makes organic content other people's problem; gives
  sponsors (TapeGeeks, GearTOP) activation beyond logo placement.
- **Objections:** O16 (team sign-up?), community trust (peer proof).
- **Production cost:** Low. Costs ~5 comped bibs. Needs graphic asset +
  posting cadence.
- **Confidence:** **Hypothesis** — UGC hashtag campaigns have a wide
  distribution of outcomes; need a minimum viable audience to seed it. Risky
  as a first test; better as a second-wave amplifier.

## Patterns to avoid (anti-patterns)

- **Generic countdown tiles.** "30 days to go!" with no stakes. Pattern D
  works only because it has a real deadline and dollar amount.
- **Stock-photo running imagery.** Kills trust for a community-built event.
  Only use real Bronte Runners members or hand-shot course content.
- **Overly polished hero videos.** On first-person platforms (IG Reels,
  TikTok, FB Reels), polish reads as corporate and reduces reach.
- **Race-expo booth photos with no face.** A tabletop of flyers doesn't
  convert — a person *at* the booth inviting someone does.
