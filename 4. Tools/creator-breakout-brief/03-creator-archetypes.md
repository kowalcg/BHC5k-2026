# 03 · Creator Archetypes — Who Tells the Story

> Stage 3 of 5. The Breakout Kit's core move: map patterns to *roles*, not
> rosters. An archetype is a type of person, not a named human. Once we know
> the roles, we cast the people.

## Why archetypes, not names

If you start with "who do we know?" you end up with whoever's available — and
your content ends up whatever *they* wanted to make. If you start with "what
role does this pattern need?" you end up with a cast list, and then you go
recruit. This matters most because we're doing this mostly organic with no
paid creator budget — we can't buy our way around casting.

## The six archetypes we need

Each archetype is defined by: **who they are**, **what only they can say**,
**which patterns they fit**, and **who on our actual team could fill the
role**.

---

### 🟥 A1 — The Peer Finisher
*"I'm one of you, and I'm doing this."*

- **Who:** A relatable first-time or returning 5K runner in the exact
  demographic we're trying to convert (35–55, local, not a "runner"
  previously, slightly nervous, earnest). Not an athlete.
- **What only they can say:** "I used to think 5K was impossible. I'm doing
  Bronte Harbour Classic anyway." That sentence from Greg K. or a coach
  means nothing. From a Bronte Runners member in month 3 of couch-to-5K, it
  moves the needle.
- **Patterns they fit:** **B** (training diary), H (UGC challenge).
- **Objections they kill:** O1, O2, O4, D1.
- **Who fills it:** Recruit 1–2 Bronte Runners Club members who joined in
  the last 6 months. Liz / Patricia to nominate. Needs a weekly content
  commitment (5 min / week).
- **Risk:** If their life gets busy and they drop the series, the whole
  pattern breaks. Recruit **two**, not one, so one continuing is enough.

---

### 🟥 A2 — The Dad Hero
*"This is the Father's Day I wanted."*

- **Who:** A dad of a 4–10-year-old in Oakville/Burlington/Mississauga.
  Warm, not macho. Kid visibly excited. Dad is slightly out-of-shape, not
  an ultra-marathoner.
- **What only they can say:** The Father's Day framing lands emotionally
  only when it's earnest, *with* the kid, on camera. A race-director voice-
  over saying "Run with your kids!" is noise; a real dad running the Kids
  1K with his daughter is the entire product.
- **Patterns they fit:** **C** (dad-kid duo training), Father's Day-eve
  urgency posts.
- **Objections they kill:** O8, O9, O10, D2, D6 (the charity angle lands
  here, not anywhere else).
- **Who fills it:** Oakville Dads Charity is the obvious recruiting channel
  — ask for one featured family. Fall-back: ask Bronte Runners Club for a
  member who's a dad and has a photogenic-age kid.
- **Risk:** Kids get camera-shy. Need the dad to be the lead, kid as co-
  star. Avoid exploiting the kid.

---

### 🟧 A3 — The Local Insider
*"I've lived here 20 years. Let me show you the harbour the way locals see it."*

- **Who:** A long-time Bronte Village resident, Bronte Runners veteran, or
  small-business owner. Knows the coffee shops, the hidden trails, the
  history of the harbour. Middle-aged, unhurried, authoritative in a warm
  way.
- **What only they can say:** Location-insider credibility. The GTA-wider
  audience (O14, O15, D4) needs to feel they've *already* been to Bronte
  before they register.
- **Patterns they fit:** A (cinematic course reveal with insider VO), F
  (local business takeover), G (inaugural build-out).
- **Objections they kill:** O14, O15, D4, O12.
- **Who fills it:** Bronte Runners Club long-term members (Liz, Patricia,
  Gus plausible); Peach Coffee Shop owner; Second Cup manager if they're
  willing. One insider per piece of content is enough.
- **Risk:** Tone drifts into tourism-board promo if over-scripted. Keep it
  phone-shot, one-take.

---

### 🟧 A4 — The Race Director
*"I'm responsible for this. Here's what we're building."*

- **Who:** Greg K. himself. The authority voice. Used *sparingly*.
- **What only they can say:** Trust-and-credibility-killer-blows. "I've
  personally walked every metre of this course." "Our timing partner is the
  same company that times Toronto Waterfront Marathon." "Town of Oakville
  approved this in writing on [date]." These sentences address O5 (chaos
  fear) and O12 (first-year mess fear) like nothing else can.
- **Patterns they fit:** **G** (inaugural build-out), D (urgency posts when
  paired with "from the race director" framing).
- **Objections they kill:** O5, O12, O13, T2, T5.
- **Who fills it:** Greg K. Only Greg K. Nobody else has the standing.
- **Risk:** Over-used, becomes corporate. Budget: ~1 post per 2 weeks.
  Never the CTA-of-last-resort.

---

### 🟨 A5 — The Coach Educator
*"Here's exactly how to train for a 5K in 8 weeks without getting injured."*

- **Who:** A credentialed running coach or experienced pacer. Glasses-on,
  no-nonsense, explainer energy.
- **What only they can say:** Beginner-friendly training content that
  doubles as a soft-sell for the race. "Sign up for a goal race in 8 weeks"
  is a coaching cliché that happens to route directly to us.
- **Patterns they fit:** Training-tip series (already in marketing tracker
  as MKT-005 "Tip of the Week"), carousel posts on FB.
- **Objections they kill:** O2, O7, D5, D1.
- **Who fills it:** Pace Performance coaches (organizing partner). Bronte
  Runners Club run leaders. Potentially a Kids 1K coach for the family-
  audience version.
- **Risk:** Comes off lecture-y. Keep each post to one tip with a
  specific, testable takeaway ("do this on your next Tuesday run").

---

### 🟦 A6 — The Scenic Storyteller
*"This place. This morning. This race."*

- **Who:** A visual-first creator — someone comfortable with a drone, a
  gimbal, a GoPro, and a tight edit. Less interested in being on camera
  than in making the place look mythic.
- **What only they can say:** Aesthetic-first content that earns saves and
  shares without needing a narrative. Works because the location *is*
  photogenic (waterfront, sunrise, marina, historic village).
- **Patterns they fit:** **A** (drone reveal), **E** (POV course run), G
  (build-out B-roll).
- **Objections they kill:** O14, O15, D4 — plus makes *every* other
  archetype's content look better as B-roll.
- **Who fills it:** Myles (already volunteered for video per MKT-004).
  Potentially a Bronte Runners member with a good iPhone and an eye.
- **Risk:** Becoming the bottleneck. Scenic content takes the longest to
  produce per post. Budget for 1–2 hero pieces, not weekly output.
